# Teacher Shortage: A Persistent Crisis

The problem of teacher shortage has become more acute as school districts across the country struggle to fill classrooms with qualified instructors. Many observers attribute the crisis to low pay and heavy workloads, but the deeper causes may lie in the changing expectations that society places on the profession.

Teachers, who once enjoyed considerable autonomy in the classroom, now find themselves accountable to standardized tests, parental demands, and administrative oversight. The result, according to a recent survey, is that nearly one in five new teachers leaves the profession within their first three years. This attrition rate, which has remained stubbornly high since the 1990s, shows little sign of improvement.

Yet the picture is not entirely bleak. In some regions, innovative recruitment programs, coupled with stronger mentorship for novice teachers, have begun to reverse the trend. Whether such measures can be scaled nationwide, however, depends less on funding than on a fundamental change in how the public values those who educate its children.
