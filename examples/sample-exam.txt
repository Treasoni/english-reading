Part A
Directions: Read the following four texts. Answer the questions below each text by choosing A, B, C or D.

Text 1

A history of long and effortless success can be a dreadful handicap, but, if properly handled, it may become a driving force.

When the United States entered just such a glowing period after the end of the Second World War, it had a market eight times larger than any competitor, giving its industries unparalleled economies of scale.

America and Americans were prosperous beyond the dreams of the Europeans and Asians whose economies the war had destroyed.

21. The U.S. achieved its predominance after World War II because
[A] it had made painstaking efforts towards this goal
[B] its domestic market was eight times larger than before
[C] the war had destroyed the economies of most potential competitors
[D] the unparalleled size of its workforce had given an impetus to its economy

22. What can be inferred from the passage?
[A] A long history of success always brings effortless progress.
[B] Intense competition may contribute to economic progress.
[C] The revival of the economy depends entirely on foreign markets.
[D] Industrial confidence has nothing to do with economic change.

Text 2

Some people believe that standardized tests are the fairest way to measure learning, while others argue that they reward narrow training rather than genuine understanding.

The debate matters because schools often change their teaching practices according to what is measured, which means that a test can quietly shape the classroom long before anyone notices.

26. The passage mainly discusses
[A] the influence of testing on education
[B] the history of sports training
[C] the decline of public libraries
[D] the popularity of travel writing

27. The word "shape" most probably means
[A] form or influence
[B] hide completely
[C] copy mechanically
