# How to Use Humor in Talks

## 文章原文

⑴ If you intend using **humor** in your talk to make people smile, you must know how to identify **shared experiences** and problems. ⑵ Your humor must be relevant to the **audience** and should help to show them that you are one of them or that you understand their situation and are in **sympathy** with their point of view. ⑶ Depending on whom you are addressing, the problems will be different. ⑷ If you are talking to a group of managers, you may refer to the **disorganized** methods of their secretaries; alternatively if you are addressing secretaries, you may want to comment on their disorganized bosses.

> [!abstract]- 长难句分析（⑵）
> **原句**：Your humor must be relevant to the **audience** and should help to show them that you are one of them or that you understand their situation and are in **sympathy** with their point of view.
>
> **主干提取**：
> - **S**：Your humor
> - **V₁**：must be
> - **P (表语)**：relevant
> - **V₂**：should help
> - **O**：them
> - **OC (宾补)**：to show + 宾从
>
> 简化主干：Your humor must be relevant to... and should help to show them that... or that...
>
> **修饰成分**：
>
> | 类型 | 引导词/结构 | 修饰对象 |
> |------|------------|----------|
> | 介词短语 | to the audience | 表语 relevant |
> | 不定式 | to show them that... or that... | 宾语 them 的补足语 |
> | 宾语从句 ① | that you are one of them | 动词 show 的宾语 |
> | 并列连词 | or | 连接两个宾语从句 |
> | 宾语从句 ② | that you understand... and are in sympathy with... | 动词 show 的宾语（与①并列） |
> | 并列谓语 | understand their situation **and** are in sympathy with their point of view | 主语 you（宾从②内部） |
> | 介词短语 | in sympathy with their point of view | 系动词 are 的表语 |
>
> **结构图解**：
> ```
> 并列主句:
> ├── 分句1: Your humor must be relevant to the audience
> │     └── 介短: to the audience → 修饰表语 relevant
> │
> └── 分句2: (Your humor) should help to show them that... or that...
>       ├── OC: to show them + 宾从
>       │     ├── 宾从①: that you are one of them
>       │     └── 宾从②: that you understand their situation
>       │           and are in sympathy with their point of view
>       │           └── 介短: in sympathy with their point of view → 表语
> ```
>
> **参考译文**：你的幽默必须与**听众**相关，应当有助于向他们表明你是他们中的一员，或者表明你理解他们的处境并且**认同**他们的观点。
>
> **考点提示**：
> - **并列宾语从句的省略**：`or` 连接两个 `that` 宾语从句，第二个 `that` 不可省略（介词后除外，但 show 为动词）。
> - **不定式作宾补**：`help sb to do sth` 中 `to` 可省，但 `show them to be / to understand` 此类感官/使役动词不定式作宾补时，结构需完整。
> - **并列谓语的识别**：宾从②内 `understand... and are in sympathy with...` 为同一主语 `you` 的并列谓语。
> - **介词搭配**：`in sympathy with`（同情/赞同）是固定搭配，写作中可替代 `agree with` 提升表达档次。

Here is an example, which I heard at a nurses' convention, of a story, which works well because the audience all shared the same view of doctors. A man arrives in heaven and is being shown around by St. Peter. He seems wonderful **accommodations**, beautiful gardens, sunny weather, and so on. Everyone is very peaceful, polite and friendly until, waiting in a line for lunch, the new arrival is suddenly pushed aside by a man in a white coat, who rushes to the head of the line, grabs his food and **stomps** over to a table by himself. ==“Who is that?”== the new arrival asked St. Peter. ==“Oh, that's God,”== came the reply, ==“but sometimes he thinks he's a doctor.”==

If you are part of the group, which you are addressing, you will be in a position to know the experiences and problems which are common to all of you and it'll be appropriate for you to make a passing remark about the **inedible** canteen food or the chairman's **notorious** bad taste in ties. With other audiences you mustn't attempt to cut in with humor as they will resent an outsider making **disparaging** remarks about their canteen or their chairman. You will be on safer ground if you stick to **scapegoats** like the Post Office or the telephone system.

If you feel awkward being humorous, you must practice so that it becomes more natural. Include a few casual and apparently **off-the-cuff** remarks which you can deliver in a relaxed and unforced manner. Often it's the **delivery** which causes the audience to smile, so speak slowly and remember that a raised eyebrow or an unbelieving look may help to show that you are making a **light-hearted** remark.

Look for the humor. It often comes from the unexpected. A twist on a familiar quote ==“If at first you don't succeed, give up”== or a play on words or on a situation. Search for **exaggeration** and **understatements**. Look at your talk and pick out a few words or sentences which you can turn about and inject with humor.
