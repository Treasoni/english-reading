# Railroad Industry Consolidation 铁路行业的整合

## Paragraph 1

> In recent years, railroads have been combining with each other, **merging** into ==supersystems==, causing heightened concerns about ==**monopoly**==. As recently as 1995, the top four railroads accounted for under 70 percent of the total **ton-miles** moved by rails. Next year, after a series of mergers is completed, just four railroads will control well over 90 percent of all the **freight** moved by major rail carriers.

近年来，铁路公司之间不断相互联合，**合并**成为==超级系统==，引发了人们对==**垄断**==的日益担忧。就在1995年，四大铁路公司的运量还占不到铁路总**吨英里数**的70%。而到明年（次年），一系列合并完成后，仅四家铁路公司就将控制主要铁路承运商90%以上的**货运量**。

> [!note] 翻译要点
> - **ton-miles（吨英里）**：运输行业的复合计量单位，表示1吨货物运输1英里。
> - **major rail carriers（主要铁路承运商）**：指规模较大的铁路运营商，与四大铁路形成对照。
> - "As recently as 1995" 强调"就在不久前的1995年"，暗示变化之快。

## Paragraph 2

> **Supporters** of the new supersystems argue that these mergers will allow for **substantial** cost reductions and better coordinated service. Any threat of monopoly, they argue, is removed by fierce competition from trucks. But many **shippers** complain that for heavy **bulk commodities** traveling long distances, such as coal, chemicals, and grain, trucking is too costly and the railroads therefore have them ==by the throat==.

新超级系统的**支持者**认为，这些合并将带来**大幅度的**成本削减和更协调的服务。他们认为，来自卡车的激烈竞争消除了任何垄断威胁。但许多**托运人**抱怨，对于煤炭、化工产品和粮食等长途运输的沉重**大宗商品**而言，公路运输成本太高，因此铁路公司==扼住了他们的咽喉==。

> [!note] 翻译要点
> - **have someone by the throat** 意为"扼住某人咽喉 / 掐住某人命脉"，形象地表达被完全控制。
> - **bulk commodities** 指散装 / 大宗商品（如煤、谷物、矿砂等），单位价值低但运量大。
> - **shipper** 在物流行业指"托运人 / 发货方"，不是"托运船只"。

## Paragraph 3

> The vast ==**consolidation**== within the rail industry means that most shippers are served by only one rail company. Railroads typically charge such ==**"captive" shippers**== 20 to 30 percent more than they do when another railroad is competing for the business. Shippers who feel they are being overcharged have the right to **appeal** to the federal government's **Surface Transportation Board** for rate relief, but the process is expensive, time consuming, and will work only in truly extreme cases.

铁路行业内部的==大规模**整合**==意味着大多数托运人只能由一家铁路公司提供服务。对于这些==**"受制的"托运人**==，铁路公司收取的费率通常比存在另一家铁路公司竞争业务时高出20%到30%。感到被多收费的托运人有权向联邦政府的**陆路运输委员会**（Surface Transportation Board）**申诉**以获得费率减免，但这一过程费用高昂、耗时漫长，且只有在真正极端的情况下才会奏效。

> [!note] 翻译要点
> - **captive shipper**（受制托运人）：指因没有替代选择而被某家铁路"锁定"的客户，类似于"captive market（受控市场）"的用法。
> - **rate relief** 意为"费率减免 / 费率救济"，是受制方向监管机构请求保护的术语。
> - **Surface Transportation Board（STB）**是美国联邦的独立监管机构，主管铁路等陆路运输费率。

## Paragraph 4

> Railroads justify **rate discrimination** against captive shippers on the grounds that in the long run it reduces everyone's cost. If railroads charged all customers the same average rate, they argue, shippers who have the option of switching to trucks or other forms of transportation would do so, leaving remaining customers to **shoulder** the cost of keeping up the line. It's a theory to which many economists **subscribe**, but in practice it often leaves railroads in the position of determining which companies will **flourish** and which will fail. "Do we really want railroads to be the ==**arbiters**== of ==who wins and who loses in the marketplace==?" asks Martin Bercovici, a Washington lawyer who frequently represents shippers.

铁路公司以"从长远看能降低所有人的成本"为由，为自己对受制托运人的**费率歧视**进行辩护。他们认为，如果对所有客户都按相同平均费率收费，那些可以选择转向卡车或其他运输方式的托运人就会这么做，留下剩余的客户**承担**维持铁路运营的成本。这一理论得到许多经济学家的**认同**，但在实践中，它往往使铁路公司处于决定哪些公司**兴盛**、哪些公司衰败的位置。"我们真的想让铁路公司成为==**市场**==上==谁赢谁输的**仲裁者**==吗？"经常代表托运人的华盛顿律师马丁·伯科维西（Martin Bercovici）问道。

> [!note] 翻译要点
> - **on the grounds that** 意为"以……为由 / 鉴于"，是阅读中常考的理由引出结构。
> - **shoulder the cost** 中 shoulder 作动词，意为"承担 / 肩负"，形象生动。
> - **subscribe to** 意为"认同 / 赞同（观点、理论）"，不是"订阅"。
> - **arbiter** 意为"仲裁者 / 裁决者"，原题第54题即考查此词。
> - **arbiters of who wins and who loses** 直译"输赢的仲裁者"，意译为"决定谁赢谁输的人"。

## Paragraph 5

> Many captive shippers also worry they will soon be hit with ==a round of huge rate increases==. The railroad industry as a whole, despite its brightening fortunes, still does not earn enough to cover the cost of the **capital** it must invest to keep up with its **surging** traffic. Yet railroads continue to borrow billions to acquire one another, with ==Wall Street cheering them on==. Consider the \$10.2 billion bid by Norfolk Southern and CSX to acquire Conrail this year. Conrail's **net railway operating income** in 1996 was just \$427 million, less than half of the **carrying costs** of the transaction. Who's going to pay for the rest of the bill? Many captive shippers fear that they will, as Norfolk Southern and CSX increase their grip on the market.

许多受制托运人还担心，他们很快将面临==一轮大幅度的费率上涨==。尽管整个铁路行业前景看好，但其收入仍不足以支付其必须投入的**资本**成本，以跟上不断**激增**的运输需求。然而，铁路公司仍继续举债数十亿美元相互收购，==华尔街在一旁为之喝彩==。以今年诺福克南方铁路公司（Norfolk Southern）和CSX公司出价102亿美元收购联合铁路公司（Conrail）为例。联合铁路公司1996年的**铁路运营净收入**仅为4.27亿美元，不足该交易**资金占用成本**的一半。这笔交易的剩余费用由谁来支付？许多受制托运人担心，随着诺福克南方和CSX加强对市场的控制，最终买单的将是他们。

> [!note] 翻译要点
> - **a round of huge rate increases** 中 a round of 意为"一轮 / 一连串"，常用于涨价、减税、掌声等。
> - **Wall Street cheering them on** 直译"华尔街为他们喝彩"，意指投资银行和股票市场看好这些收购行为。
> - **carrying costs** 财务术语，指"持有资产期间的资金占用成本（如利息）"，这里特指收购交易所产生的财务费用。
> - **net railway operating income（铁路运营净收入）**：扣除运营费用后的净收益，是衡量铁路公司盈利能力的关键指标。

## Questions 题目

### 51. According to those who support mergers, railway monopoly is unlikely because

根据支持合并的人士的观点，铁路垄断不太可能发生，因为：

- [A] cost reduction is based on competition — 成本削减以竞争为基础
- [B] services call for cross-trade coordination — 服务需要跨行业协调
- [C] outside competitors will continue to exist — 外部竞争者（卡车）将继续存在
- [D] shippers will have the railway by the throat — 托运人会扼住铁路的咽喉

> [!note] 答案提示：**C**。对应第二段"Any threat of monopoly... is removed by fierce competition from trucks"。

### 52. What is many captive shippers' attitude towards the consolidation in the rail industry?

许多受制托运人对铁路行业整合的态度是：

- [A] Indifferent — 漠不关心
- [B] Supportive — 支持
- [C] Indignant — 愤慨
- [D] Apprehensive — 忧虑 / 担忧

> [!note] 答案提示：**D**。文中受制托运人"worry they will soon be hit with a round of huge rate increases"，体现忧虑情绪。

### 53. It can be inferred from paragraph 3 that

从第三段可以推断出：

- [A] shippers will be charged less without a rival railroad — 没有竞争铁路时托运人支付的费用更少
- [B] there will soon be only one railroad company nationwide — 很快将只剩一家全国性铁路公司
- [C] overcharged shippers are unlikely to appeal for rate relief — 被多收费的托运人不太可能申诉费率减免
- [D] a government board ensures fair play in railway business — 政府委员会确保铁路业的公平竞争

> [!note] 答案提示：**C**。第三段提到申诉"process is expensive, time consuming, and will work only in truly extreme cases"，因此被多收费的托运人实际上不太可能去申诉。

### 54. The word "arbiters" (line 6, paragraph 4) most probably refers to those

单词 "arbiters"（第四段第6行）最可能指：

- [A] who work as coordinators — 充当协调者的人
- [B] who function as judges — 充当裁判 / 裁决者的人
- [C] who supervise transactions — 监督交易的人
- [D] who determine the price — 决定价格的人

> [!note] 答案提示：**B**。"arbiters of who wins and who loses"直译为"输赢的仲裁者"，即决定胜负的人，对应 judges（裁判）。注意 D 选项 price 偷换了原文 marketplace（市场整体输赢）的概念。

### 55. According to the text, the cost increase in the rail industry is mainly caused by

根据文章，铁路行业成本上升主要由什么导致：

- [A] the continuing acquisition — 持续的收购
- [B] the growing traffic — 不断增长的运输量
- [C] the cheering Wall Street — 喝彩的华尔街
- [D] the shrinking market — 萎缩的市场

> [!note] 答案提示：**A**。第五段明确指出铁路公司"borrow billions to acquire one another"且资金占用成本高昂，导致行业整体成本上升。
