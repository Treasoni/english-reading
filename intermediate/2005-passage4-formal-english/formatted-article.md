## Text 4

Americans no longer expect public figures, whether in speech or in writing, to **command the English language** with skill and gift. Nor do they aspire to such command themselves. In his latest book, *Doing Our Own Thing: The Degradation of Language and Music and Why We Should, Like, Care*, John McWhorter, a linguist and controversialist of mixed liberal and conservative views, sees the triumph of 1960s **counter-culture** as responsible for the **decline of formal English**.

> [!abstract]- 长难句分析
> **原句**：Americans no longer expect public figures, whether in speech or in writing, to command the English language with skill and gift.
>
> | 成分 | 内容 |
> |------|------|
> | 主语 | Americans |
> | 谓语 | expect |
> | 宾语 | public figures |
> | 宾语补足语 | to command the English language |
> | 插入语（修饰 public figures） | whether in speech or in writing |
> | 方式状语（修饰 command） | with skill and gift |
>
> **结构图解**：
> ```
> Americans
> ├── no longer expect（谓语，表否定）
> ├── public figures（宾语）
> │   └── whether in speech or in writing（插入语，表让步）
> └── to command the English language（宾语补足语）
>     └── with skill and gift（方式状语）
> ```
>
> **参考译文**：美国人不再期望公众人物在演讲或写作中有技巧、有天赋地掌握英语。
>
> **考点提示**：
> - "whether...or..." 结构引导插入语，考研阅读中常作"无论是……还是……"理解
> - "expect sb. to do sth." 是重要搭配，expect 后接不定式作宾语补足语
> - "no longer" 否定词置于谓语前，注意否定范围

> [!abstract]- 长难句分析
> **原句**：In his latest book, *Doing Our Own Thing: The Degradation of Language and Music and Why We Should, Like, Care*, John McWhorter, a linguist and controversialist of mixed liberal and conservative views, sees the triumph of 1960s counter-culture as responsible for the decline of formal English.
>
> | 成分 | 内容 |
> |------|------|
> | 主语 | John McWhorter |
> | 同位语 | a linguist and controversialist of mixed liberal and conservative views |
> | 谓语 | sees |
> | 宾语 | the triumph of 1960s counter-culture |
> | 宾语补足语 | as responsible for the decline of formal English |
> | 状语 | In his latest book, *Doing Our Own Thing:...* |
>
> **结构图解**：
> ```
> [状语] In his latest book, *Doing Our Own Thing:...*
> [主语] John McWhorter
>   └── [同位语] a linguist and controversialist of mixed liberal and conservative views
> [谓语] sees
> [宾语] the triumph of 1960s counter-culture
> [宾补] as responsible for the decline of formal English
> ```
>
> **参考译文**：约翰·麦克沃特在其新书《做自己的事：语言和音乐的退化以及我们为什么应该——嗯——在意》中，这位兼具自由和保守观点的语言学家和辩论家，认为20世纪60年代反文化的胜利导致了正式英语的衰落。
>
> **考点提示**：
> - 主语和谓语之间被长的同位语分隔，考研阅读中常设"打断结构"题，需快速找到主谓
> - "see...as..." 视……为……，考研高频动词短语
> - 长书名含多重并列结构（"The Degradation of Language and Music and Why We Should, Like, Care"），注意并列项的划分

Blaming the permissive 1960s is nothing new, but this is not yet another criticism against the decline in education. Mr. McWhorter's academic speciality is language history and change, and he sees the gradual disappearance of "whom", for example, to be natural and no more regrettable than the loss of the **case-endings** of Old English.

> [!abstract]- 长难句分析
> **原句**：Mr. McWhorter's academic speciality is language history and change, and he sees the gradual disappearance of "whom", for example, to be natural and no more regrettable than the loss of the case-endings of Old English.
>
> | 成分 | 内容 |
> |------|------|
> | 并列分句1 — 主语 | Mr. McWhorter's academic speciality |
> | 并列分句1 — 谓语 | is |
> | 并列分句1 — 表语 | language history and change |
> | 并列分句2 — 主语 | he |
> | 并列分句2 — 谓语 | sees |
> | 并列分句2 — 宾语 | the gradual disappearance of "whom" |
> | 并列分句2 — 宾语补足语 | to be natural and no more regrettable than the loss of the case-endings of Old English |
> | 插入语 | for example |
>
> **结构图解**：
> ```
> [并列分句1]
> Mr. McWhorter's academic speciality
> └── is language history and change
> [并列连词] and
> [并列分句2]
> he
> └── sees
>     ├── the gradual disappearance of "whom"（宾语）
>     │   └── ,for example,（插入语）
>     └── to be natural and no more regrettable than...（宾补）
>         └── the loss of the case-endings of Old English（比较对象）
> ```
>
> **参考译文**：麦克沃特的学术专长是语言史和语言演变，他认为——比如"whom"一词的逐渐消失是自然的，并不比古英语中格尾的消失更令人惋惜。
>
> **考点提示**：
> - "no more...than..." 结构 = "和……一样不……"，表示前后两者都被否定，这是考研高频考点
> - "see...to be..." 为 "see...as..." 的变体，意为"认为……是……"
> - "for example" 作为插入语打断句子，阅读时可先忽略，提取主谓宾主干

While even the modestly educated sought an elevated tone when they put pen to paper before the 1960s, even the most well regarded writing since then has sought to capture spoken English on the page. Equally, in poetry, the highly personal, **performative genre** is the only form that could claim real liveliness. In both oral and written English, ==talking is triumphing over speaking, spontaneity over craft==.

> [!abstract]- 长难句分析
> **原句**：While even the modestly educated sought an elevated tone when they put pen to paper before the 1960s, even the most well regarded writing since then has sought to capture spoken English on the page.
>
> | 成分 | 内容 |
> |------|------|
> | 主句 — 主语 | even the most well regarded writing |
> | 主句 — 谓语 | has sought |
> | 主句 — 宾语 | to capture spoken English on the page |
> | 主句 — 时间状语 | since then |
> | 让步状语从句 — 主语 | even the modestly educated |
> | 让步状语从句 — 谓语 | sought |
> | 让步状语从句 — 宾语 | an elevated tone |
> | 嵌套时间状语从句 | when they put pen to paper before the 1960s |
>
> **结构图解**：
> ```
> [While 让步状语从句]
> While even the modestly educated
> └── sought an elevated tone
>     └── [嵌套时间状语从句] when they put pen to paper before the 1960s
> [主句]
> even the most well regarded writing
> ├── since then（时间状语）
> └── has sought to capture spoken English on the page
> ```
>
> **参考译文**：在20世纪60年代之前，即使是受过一般教育的人在落笔时也追求高雅的格调；而自那以后，即使是最受好评的文章也力求在书面上捕捉口语。
>
> **考点提示**：
> - "While" 在此表示对比/让步，不是"当……时"，考研阅读常考 while 的这层含义
> - "the modestly educated" = "the + adj." 表示一类人：受过一般教育的人
> - "before the 1960s" 与 "since then" 形成时间对比，是段落主旨的关键——1960s 前后写作风格的对比

Illustrated with an entertaining array of examples from both high and low culture, the trend that Mr. McWhorter documents is unmistakable. But it is less clear, to take the question of his subtitle, why we should, like, care. As a linguist, he acknowledges that all varieties of human language, including non-standard ones like Black English, can be powerfully expressive — there exists no language or dialect in the world that cannot convey complex ideas. He is not arguing, as many do, that we can no longer think straight because we do not talk proper.

> [!abstract]- 长难句分析
> **原句**：Illustrated with an entertaining array of examples from both high and low culture, the trend that Mr. McWhorter documents is unmistakable.
>
> | 成分 | 内容 |
> |------|------|
> | 主语 | the trend |
> | 定语从句（修饰 trend） | that Mr. McWhorter documents |
> | 系动词 | is |
> | 表语 | unmistakable |
> | 状语（过去分词短语） | Illustrated with an entertaining array of examples from both high and low culture |
>
> **结构图解**：
> ```
> [状语] Illustrated with an entertaining array of examples
>         from both high and low culture
>   └── 过去分词短语作状语，逻辑主语 = 主句主语 the trend
> [主语] the trend
>   └── [定语从句] that Mr. McWhorter documents
> [系动词] is
> [表语] unmistakable
> ```
>
> **参考译文**：麦克沃特先生用一系列来自高雅文化和通俗文化的有趣例子来说明，他所记录的趋势是确凿无误的。
>
> **考点提示**：
> - 句首过去分词短语作状语，其逻辑主语等于主句主语，考研阅读中需准确识别修饰关系
> - "that Mr. McWhorter documents" 为定语从句，先行词是 trend，that 在从句中作宾语
> - "unmistakable" — 前缀 un- + mistake + -able，否定前缀派生词，注意词汇推导能力

> [!abstract]- 长难句分析
> **原句**：He is not arguing, as many do, that we can no longer think straight because we do not talk proper.
>
> | 成分 | 内容 |
> |------|------|
> | 主语 | He |
> | 谓语 | is not arguing |
> | 插入语 / 方式状语从句 | as many do |
> | 宾语从句 | that we can no longer think straight because we do not talk proper |
> | 宾语从句 — 主语 | we |
> | 宾语从句 — 谓语 | can no longer think |
> | 宾语从句 — 状语 | straight |
> | 宾语从句内嵌 — 原因状语从句 | because we do not talk proper |
>
> **结构图解**：
> ```
> [主语] He
> [谓语] is not arguing
>   ├── [插入语] ,as many do,（方式状语从句）
>   └── [宾语从句] that we can no longer think straight
>         └── [原因状语从句] because we do not talk proper
> ```
>
> **参考译文**：他并没有像许多人那样认为——因为我们说话不规范，我们就再也不能正确思考了。
>
> **考点提示**：
> - "as many do" 为 as 引导的方式状语从句作插入语，可先跳过抓主干
> - 多重否定："not arguing"（不主张）+ "no longer"（不再）+ "not...proper"（不规范）= 需要理清否定层次
> - "think straight" — straight 作副词，意为"清晰地/有条理地"
> - "talk proper" — 作者有意使用不规范语法（proper 应为 properly）来呼应文章主题：正式语的退化

Russians have a deep love for their own language and carry large chunks of memorized poetry in their heads, while Italian politicians tend to elaborate speech that would seem old-fashioned to most English-speakers. Mr. McWhorter acknowledges that formal language is not strictly necessary, and proposes no radical education reforms — he is really grieving over the loss of something beautiful more than useful. We now take our English "on **paper plates instead of china**". A shame, perhaps, but probably an inevitable one.
